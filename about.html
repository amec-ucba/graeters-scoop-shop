Stubborn Since 1870

Graeter's has made ice cream in Cincinnati the same slow way for more than 150 years.

Two Carts and a French Pot

In 1870, Louis Charles Graeter started selling ice cream on the streets of Cincinnati from two carts. He made every batch by hand in French pots.

In 1900, Louis and his wife, Regina, opened their first shop at 967 East McMillan Street in Walnut Hills. The family lived upstairs, made ice cream and candy in the back, and sold it out front.

After Louis died in a streetcar accident in 1919, Regina kept the business going with her sons, Wilmer and Paul. She opened a second shop in Hyde Park in 1922, and in 1934, during the Depression, bought a factory on Reading Road to make all of the ice cream in one place.

Stubbornly, Regina decided to continue making ice cream the best way she knew how.

Still Made in French Pots

Graeter's says it's one of the last ice cream makers in America still using the French pot process. Small batches spin in a chilled pot, which makes the ice cream dense and leaves room for big chocolate chunks.
